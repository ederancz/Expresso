# Ion channels and Ca²⁺-handling genes shaping pyramidal-cell excitability

Companion to `excitability_genes.yaml`. Scope: **neocortical pyramidal cells**,
somatic and dendritic, sub- and suprathreshold, including subthreshold resonance.
Interneurons excluded. Granularity collapsed to the physiologically dominant
subunits in pyramidal cells.

The gene list is organised into three families (primary conductances, auxiliary
subunits, Ca²⁺ handling), each split into a **prominent** tier (direct, strong
evidence in pyramidal cells) and a **plausible** tier (expressed or mechanistically
likely but weaker/indirect support).

---

## 1. Mechanism narrative

### Resting potential and input resistance
The resting membrane potential and apparent input resistance are set by a balance
of inwardly rectifying K⁺ channels (Kir2 family), two-pore "leak" K⁺ channels
(K2P: TREK/TASK/TWIK), and a standing inward h-current. In mouse frontal cortex
pyramidal neurons, dendritic excitability emerges from the interaction of HCN, Kir2,
and leak K⁺ conductances, which together dominate the subthreshold input
conductance. These conductances also determine the membrane time constant and
therefore the temporal window for synaptic integration.

### Subthreshold dynamics and resonance
Two currents dominate the perithreshold subthreshold regime in pyramidal cells:
the persistent Na⁺ current (I_NaP, an *amplifying* current carried by Nav1.2/Nav1.6)
and two *resonant* currents — the h-current (I_h, HCN1/HCN2) and the M-current
(I_M, Kv7.2/7.3/7.5). Resonance arises from the pairing of a slow resonant current
(which acts as a high-pass filter) with passive membrane capacitance (a low-pass
filter), yielding a band-pass impedance profile. I_h supports H-resonance at rest
and in dendrites (typically ~1–5 Hz), while I_M supports M-resonance in the theta
band at potentials near spike threshold. I_NaP does not generate resonance itself
but amplifies the voltage response, sharpening the resonance peak; pharmacological
removal of TTX-sensitive Na⁺ attenuates resonance in pyramidal cells specifically.
HCN-channel block (ZD7288) abolishes H-resonance entirely.

### Spike generation and the axon initial segment
Action potentials initiate in the distal axon initial segment (AIS), where Nav1.6
(Scn8a) predominates and sets the lowest threshold; Nav1.2 (Scn2a) occupies the
proximal AIS and somatodendritic membrane, where it supports backpropagation.
Threshold and first-spike latency are tuned by AIS-localised Kv1.1/1.2 (D-type
current) and Kv7 (M-current). Resurgent and persistent Na⁺ components — shaped by
Navβ subunits (notably Scn4b) and intracellular FGFs (Fgf12/13/14) — promote
repetitive and high-frequency firing.

### Repolarisation, afterhyperpolarisations, and adaptation
Spike repolarisation is driven by delayed-rectifier Kv2.1 (somatic, enabling
sustained high-frequency firing) and by BK channels (Kcnma1), which are activated
within ~500 µs of the spike peak by adjacent Ca²⁺ channels and mediate the fast AHP.
The medium AHP is carried by apamin-sensitive SK channels (Kcnn1/2/3); the slow AHP
is Ca²⁺-dependent and involves Na⁺-activated K⁺ channels (Slack/Slick) and
hippocalcin-linked signalling. A-type K⁺ current (Kv4.2 + KChIP2 + DPPX) controls
interspike interval, dendritic backpropagation amplitude, and synaptic integration,
with a characteristic increasing-density gradient along the apical dendrite.

### Dendritic regenerative events
Apical dendrites of layer-5 pyramidal cells support Ca²⁺ spikes generated by HVA
(Cav1 L-type, Cav2.3 R-type, with Cav2.1/2.2 contributing) and LVA (Cav3 T-type)
channels, plus NMDA-receptor-dependent spikes in basal/tuft dendrites. BK channels
raise the threshold for dendritic Ca²⁺ electrogenesis and gate the coupling between
somatic firing and the distal initiation zone. SK channels exert a more complex,
context-dependent influence: in basal dendrites they can lower the NMDA-spike
threshold via R-type Ca²⁺ channels while suppressing spike amplitude. Intracellular
Ca²⁺ stores (RyR2, IP3R1) amplify dendritic Ca²⁺ signals, and extrusion/reuptake
machinery (PMCA, SERCA, NCX) plus mobile buffers (calbindin, calmodulin) set the
amplitude and time course of the Ca²⁺ transients that gate all Ca²⁺-activated
conductances.

### Scope notes / decisions for the executor

- **Two-tier split is a judgement call**, not a hard boundary. The "plausible" tier
  is deliberately generous (maximalist brief). Filter by expression data (e.g.
  Allen / scRNA-seq cortical pyramidal clusters) before any functional analysis.
- **TRIP8b appears under both `Trip8b` and `Pex5l`** — same protein, one mouse
  symbol (`Pex5l`). Deduplicate to `Pex5l` if mapping to ENSMUSG.
- **TARPs (Cacng2/8)** are primarily AMPA-receptor auxiliary subunits; included only
  because of secondary Ca²⁺-channel modulation. Drop if scope is strictly intrinsic.
- **Ca²⁺ buffers and calmodulin** are not conductances; included per the maximalist
  request. They gate Ca²⁺-activated channels indirectly. First to cut if needed.
- **Muscle/developmental Na⁺ subunits** (Scn1a, Scn3a) are low in mature pyramids —
  kept in the plausible tier for completeness.
- All gene picks collapsed to **dominant pyramidal subunits**; e.g. Kcnq2/3/5 not
  the full Kcnq1–5, Kcnd2 prioritised over Kcnd1.
