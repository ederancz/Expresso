# Receptor modulation of pyramidal-cell excitability and synaptic release

Companion to `receptor_query_config.yaml`. Scope: **all entries** in the config, including commented-out lines (ionotropic glutamate Grin/Gria/Grik; GABA-A Gabra/b/g/d…). Commented entries are currently inactive in the analysis pipeline but are included here for receptor-system completeness; they are grouped and labelled accordingly in the table. Coverage spans **neocortical pyramidal cells**, layers 2/3 and 5, with emphasis on areas VISp, VISpm, VISam, and RSPagl. Both presynaptic compartments are distinguished throughout: autoreceptors on pyramidal axon terminals (control of pyramidal output) and heteroreceptors on afferent terminals (control of input onto pyramidal cells). Where a receptor is reliably expressed in interneurons or glia rather than pyramidal cells, that is stated explicitly and the pyramidal-cell frame is not imposed. Every row carries an evidence tag: **[P]** measured in cortical pyramidal neurons; **[E:region]** measured in pyramidal neurons of another region; **[I]** inferred from G-protein coupling and expression, no direct pyramidal recording. Effector conductances cross-referenced throughout — Kcnq2/3/5 (M-current), Kcnn1/2/3 (SK), Kcnj2 (Kir2), Kcnj3/6 (GIRK), Hcn1/2 (I_h), Cacna1c/b/a (Cav), Kcnma1 (BK), Kcnd2 (Kv4.2 A-current), Scn2a/8a (Nav), Ryr2, Itpr1 — are defined in `excitability_genes.yaml` and `excitability_genes.md`.

## 1. Mechanism narrative

### 1.1 Gq/11 receptors: M-current and SK suppression, Kir2 closure, dendritic facilitation

Receptors in this class — M1/M3/M5 mAChR (Chrm1/3/5), α1-AR (Adra1a/b/d), 5-HT2A/B/C (Htr2a/b/c), Group I mGluR (Grm1/5), H1 (Hrh1), orexin (Hcrtr1/2), CCK-B (Cckbr), NK1–3 (Tacr1–3), and most neuropeptide receptors — couple to Gq/11 and activate PLCβ, generating IP3 and DAG and depleting membrane PIP2. The dominant consequence on pyramidal-cell intrinsic excitability is collapse of the Kcnq2/3/5 M-current, which requires PIP2 for activation. PIP2 hydrolysis releases the M-current from its phospholipid gating partner, suppressing the slow K+ conductance mediating spike-threshold accommodation and the post-burst AHP. The result is depolarisation, increased input resistance, elimination of spike-frequency adaptation, and a shift toward tonic or bursting firing.

Two additional conductances are targeted by the same Gq/11 signal. M1 mAChR activation closes Kcnj2 Kir2 channels via PIP2 depletion, increasing temporal summation of distally evoked EPSPs and making the cell responsive to high-frequency rather than isolated afferent volleys. Separately, Gq/11 receptors suppress the Kcnn2 SK medium-AHP via downstream PKA/CK2 phosphorylation that reduces SK Ca²+ sensitivity, with M1 mAChR-driven SK2 suppression shown to gate long-term intrinsic plasticity in L2/3 somatosensory cortex pyramidal neurons.

At the dendritic level, Gq/11 activation facilitates regenerative Ca²+ and NMDA spikes. In L5 barrel-cortex pyramidal neurons, combined activation of nicotinic and M1 muscarinic receptors by ACh enhances EPSCs, reduces IPSCs via M2, and shifts cells to a Ca²+ spike/burst mode through Cacna1c L-type and NMDAR activation. The same Gq cascade in 5-HT2A/C-activated PFC pyramidal neurons runs an opposing branch: PLCβ → IP3 → calcineurin → inhibition of Cacna1c, and PKC-dependent reduction of persistent and transient Scn2a/8a Na+ current, suppressing backpropagating APs and dendritic excitability. These contrasting outcomes reflect the same coupling acting on different effector cascades at different receptor densities.

### 1.2 Gi/Go receptors: GIRK activation, Cav inhibition, ↓cAMP — the dominant presynaptic inhibitory logic

Receptors in this class — M2/M4 mAChR (Chrm2/4), α2-AR (Adra2a/b/c), 5-HT1A/B/D/F (Htr1a/b/d/f), Group II/III mGluR (Grm2/3/4/6/7/8), GABA-B (Gabbr1/2), A1 adenosine (Adora1), CB1 (Cnr1), D2-like (Drd2/3/4), μ/δ/κ opioids, SST receptors, NPY receptors, H3 (Hrh3), and most neuropeptide receptors — activate Gi/Go. Their three principal effector arms are: (1) Gβγ-mediated activation of GIRK channels (Kcnj3/6), producing a slow shunting K+ current; (2) Gα-mediated inhibition of adenylyl cyclase, reducing cAMP and PKA activity; (3) Gβγ-mediated inhibition of N-type (Cacna1b) and P/Q-type (Cacna1a) Ca²+ channels at presynaptic terminals.

Postsynaptically, GIRK activation generates the slow IPSP in cortical pyramidal neurons. The cAMP reduction has an important consequence on Hcn1/2: reduced cAMP shifts HCN activation leftward, decreasing I_h, which hyperpolarises the membrane but paradoxically increases Rin and temporal summation of distal EPSPs. This α2-AR mechanism in PFC L5/6 pyramidal neurons uses a PLC-PKC pathway independent of cAMP, illustrating that Gi coupling does not always operate solely via adenylyl cyclase. Presynaptically, the Gi/Go effector logic is the principal gate on release at virtually all central synapses: Gβγ inhibits Cav, and reduced cAMP → ↓PKA → dephosphorylation of RIM1α drives presynaptic LTD, the shared mechanism of mGluR2 and A1 adenosine receptor activation. CB1 operates the same Gi/o-Cav-cAMP circuit retrogradely: 2-AG is synthesised postsynaptically from DAG (a Gq/11 PLCβ product) and acts on presynaptic CB1, coupling postsynaptic Gq activation directly to presynaptic Gi inhibition.

### 1.3 Gs receptors: ↑cAMP → HCN depolarising shift, Cav facilitation

Receptors in this class — β1/β2-AR (Adrb1/2), 5-HT4/6/7 (Htr4/6/7), D1-like (Drd1/5), A2A (Adora2a), H2 (Hrh2), CRF1/2, VIP/PACAP — raise cAMP via Gs-coupled adenylyl cyclase. Consequences on pyramidal intrinsic excitability: HCN activation shifted rightward → increased I_h at rest → depolarisation with decreased Rin and faster membrane time-constant; PKA phosphorylation of Cacna1c/1e enhancing Ca²+ influx; and PKA-mediated enhancement of vesicle fusion probability. β1-AR drives I_h depolarisation in mPFC L5 pyramidal neurons via a membrane-delimited Gβγ pathway rather than the canonical Gs/cAMP/PKA route. Dopamine D1-like receptors at deep-layer PFC pyramidal dendritic spines illustrate the dual-sided effect of cAMP-HCN: at optimal cAMP, D1-cAMP-HCN tunes network connections; at elevated cAMP (stress), it collapses network firing. This places D1/cAMP/HCN in opposition to the α2A/GIRK-HCN axis — a push-pull architecture directly relevant to the ACh/NE project.

### 1.4 The HCN axis as an ACh/NE convergence point

Hcn1 and Hcn2 integrate signals from four receptor systems active in the study areas. β-AR (Adrb1, via Gβγ or cAMP) and D1-like (Drd1, via cAMP) shift HCN activation rightward, increasing I_h and speeding membrane dynamics. α2-AR (Adra2a, via PLC-PKC) shifts HCN activation leftward, decreasing I_h, increasing Rin and temporal summation. M1/M3 mAChR (via Gq/PIP2/PKC) suppresses Kcnq M-current and Kcnn2 SK, producing a state qualitatively similar to, but mechanistically distinct from, α2-AR-driven Ih suppression. The Allen scRNA-seq and MERFISH expression data defining which receptors are present on individual pyramidal supertypes will be the decisive filter for which of these convergences is physiologically operative in VISp/VISpm/VISam/RSPagl.

### 1.5 Ionotropic glutamate receptors: NMDA coincidence detection, AMPA fast excitation, kainate presynaptic metabotropic signalling

NMDA receptors (Grin*) require both glutamate (binding GluN2 subunits) and glycine/D-serine (binding GluN1/GluN3) for activation, and are gated further by voltage-dependent Mg²+ block that is relieved around −30 mV. This coincidence-detection property makes NMDARs the canonical Hebbian plasticity trigger. In cortical pyramidal neurons, the dominant synaptic NMDAR contains Grin1 (obligate) plus Grin2a and/or Grin2b. Grin2b-containing receptors have slower deactivation, higher Ca²+ permeability, and sensitivity to ifenprodil; Grin2a-containing receptors deactivate faster and predominate at mature synapses following the well-known GluN2B-to-GluN2A subunit switch during critical-period development. NMDAR Ca²+ influx triggers CaMKII activation, which drives AMPAR insertion (LTP) and mediates dendritic Ca²+ signalling coupled to the Ca²+-activated conductances in excitability_genes.yaml (Kcnma1 BK, Kcnn2 SK, and Ca²+ stores via Ryr2/Itpr1). Grin3a/3b subunits form inhibitory glycine-binding subunits that reduce current amplitude and Ca²+ permeability of the receptor when co-assembled with Grin1; Grin3a is expressed developmentally and downregulated by synaptic activity in adult cortex.

AMPA receptors (Gria*) mediate the fast component of EPSCs. The decisive structural determinant of AMPAR Ca²+ permeability is Q/R RNA editing at position 607 of Gria2: edited Gria2 (dominant form in adult cortex, >99% edited) confers Ca²+ impermeability on any heteromeric AMPAR containing it; it also removes inward rectification by preventing intracellular spermine block. Most cortical pyramidal AMPARs are GluA1/GluA2 or GluA2/GluA3 heteromers. Synaptic AMPAR number and conductance are regulated by phosphorylation of Gria1 (S845-PKA → insertion; S831-CaMKII → increased channel conductance) and by TARP auxiliary subunits (Cacng2/stargazin, Cacng8), which modulate gating kinetics, subcellular localisation, and pharmacology. LTD involves PKC-PICK1-driven Gria2 endocytosis.

Kainate receptors (Grik*) mediate a slower EPSC component than AMPARs. Grik2 (GluK2) and the high-affinity subunits Grik4/5 are the principal expressed forms in cortex. A distinctive feature is their dual ionotropic/metabotropic signalling capacity at presynaptic terminals: Grik1 (GluK1) at GABAergic terminals can both ionotropically excite and, via Gi/o-PKC, inhibit GABA release depending on activation state. This makes kainate receptors unusual among ionotropic glutamate receptors in having a presynaptic modulatory capacity that partly parallels mGluRs.

### 1.6 GABA-A: phasic and tonic Cl⁻ inhibition, subunit diversity, shunting

GABA-A receptors are pentameric Cl⁻ channels (with minor HCO3⁻ permeability) whose subunit composition determines kinetics, pharmacology, and subcellular targeting. In adult cortical pyramidal cells, two functionally distinct GABA-A populations operate.

Synaptic (phasic) GABA-A receptors are activated by quantal GABA release and mediate IPSCs. Dominant subunit combinations in pyramidal synapses are α1βγ2 (perisomatic, fast-decaying, zolpidem-sensitive), α2βγ2 (soma/AIS, the target of chandelier cell synapses), and α3βγ2 (dendritic, slower decay). Gabrg2 (γ2) is required for synaptic clustering via gephyrin and for benzodiazepine binding; Gabrb2 is the most abundant β subunit and obligate for surface trafficking. Somatic IPSCs (PV basket/chandelier cell input) are dominated by fast α1β2γ2 receptors; dendritic IPSCs (SST/Martinotti cell input) often involve α2/α3.

Extrasynaptic (tonic) GABA-A receptors are activated by ambient extracellular GABA and mediate a persistent inhibitory conductance. Gabra5 (α5-containing) is the principal tonic inhibition subunit in cortical pyramidal cell dendrites; α5-knockout mice show enhanced LTP and improved spatial memory. Gabrd (δ subunit) contributes a second tonic pool, typically with Gabra4, with high GABA affinity, benzodiazepine insensitivity, and strong modulation by neurosteroids and ethanol. Together, the tonic conductance set by α5/δ-containing receptors determines the background level of dendritic shunting and the integration window for excitatory inputs onto pyramidal cells.

The ρ subunits (Gabrr1/2/3) form GABA-C (GABA-Aρ) receptors with distinct pharmacology — bicuculline-insensitive, benzodiazepine-insensitive — and are expressed predominantly in the retina; cortical pyramidal expression is negligible.

### 1.7 Ionotropic receptors: nicotinic, 5-HT3, P2X, glycine — direct currents without G-protein intermediary

Neuronal nicotinic receptors (Chrna2–7, Chrnb2–4) assembled as α4β2 heteromers or α7 homomers are the principal ionotropic ACh channels in neocortex. Both subtypes generate inward cation currents. α7 homomers have high Ca²+ permeability and are found at presynaptic glutamatergic terminals (enhancing release) and on pyramidal dendrites. α4β2 heteromers across L2–L6 produce slower, sustained depolarisation; β2 + α4 are the most abundant transcripts. Layer I interneurons (primarily α7 + α4β2) are preferentially excited by nicotinic agonists, producing circuit-level disinhibition of pyramids that may dominate over direct nicotinic depolarisation of pyramidal cells. The 5-HT3 receptor (Htr3a/b) is an ionotropic cation channel marking VIP/Reelin interneurons; postsynaptic expression in cortical pyramidal cells is negligible. P2X receptors (P2rx4, P2rx7) mediate ATP-gated cation currents in CNS neurons; P2rx7 forms large pores at very high ATP (a pathological condition). Glycine receptors (Glra1–4, Glrb) mediate Cl⁻ currents and are expressed at very low levels in mature cortical pyramidal cells; cortical expression is extrasynaptic and functionally marginal.

### 1.8 Presynaptic release control: auto- versus heteroreceptors, Cav/cAMP/Kir routes

For the ACh system: M2 (Chrm2) autoreceptors on cholinergic varicosities suppress ACh release via Gi/Cav. On pyramidal glutamatergic axon terminals, M2/M4 heteroreceptors suppress glutamate release, while M1/M3 can facilitate it via PKC. For the NE system: Adra2a dominates as the NE autoreceptor on locus coeruleus terminals; α2A/2C heteroreceptors on glutamatergic afferents suppress excitatory input; presynaptic α1-AR facilitates glutamate release. The endocannabinoid retrograde mechanism (CB1/Cnr1), Group II/III mGluR autoreceptors, GABA-B, A1 adenosine, and SST2 heteroreceptors all share the Gi/Go → Cav/cAMP presynaptic inhibition motif. Presynaptic NMDARs at some cortical synapses (containing Grin2b) facilitate release by depolarising terminals and promoting Ca²+ entry; this is distinct from the postsynaptic Mg²+-gated coincidence detector role.

## 3. Presynaptic modulation of V2M afferents, organised by source area

The catalogue in §2 is organised by transmitter. This section reorganises its *presynaptic* half by **afferent pathway**, using the V2M input map of Galloni, Ye & Rancz (2022) [24]. The organising logic: a heteroreceptor protein is synthesised in the projection neuron's soma — in its source area, at its layer of origin — and trafficked to the terminal in V2M, where it gates that specific input. The transcriptome of each projecting population therefore predicts which neuromodulators control that pathway on arrival. Because projection-specific transcriptomes (e.g. retrogradely labelled V2M-projecting ORB neurons) are largely unpublished, this section is an explicit **hypothesis layer**: it fuses the measured V2M connectivity [P], the established pathway-class pharmacology of cortical neuromodulation [E], and inference from each source population's identity [I]. It is built to generate predictions to test against the Allen/Zhuang/Vizgen expression data for each source area — not to report measured projection-specific receptor counts.

V2M = VISpm + VISam + RSPagl (Allen CCFv3); Galloni treats the three as one target because their long-range axonal inputs do not differ substantially [24]. Monosynaptic rabies tracing from Colgalt2-Cre thick-tufted L5 (ttL5) neurons identified seven dominant inputs, each examined by subcellular optogenetic mapping (sCRACM). Most cortical inputs originate in L5/infragranular layers; **ORB is the exception, projecting almost exclusively from L2/3**, while VISp projects mostly from L5.

### 3.1 The thalamocortical–corticocortical dissociation (master axis)

The single most useful organising result is the dissociation of Gil, Connors & Amitai (1997) [25]: in neocortex, thalamocortical (TC) and intracortical/corticocortical (IC) excitatory terminals are gated by *different* presynaptic neuromodulator receptors. Only IC terminals are suppressed by GABA-B (Gabbr1/2); only TC terminals are enhanced by nicotinic receptors; muscarinic receptors suppress both. This maps directly onto the Galloni input classes. The five cortical inputs to V2M — VISp, local V2M, RSPg, ACA, ORB — are IC-type, and are therefore predicted to be GABA-B- and muscarinic-suppressed but *not* nicotinically facilitated. The two thalamic inputs — LP and ATN — are TC-type, predicted to be nicotinically facilitated and GABA-B-insensitive. This single dissociation already assigns opposite cholinergic signs to the cortical versus thalamic streams converging on a V2M ttL5 neuron.

The nicotinic facilitation of thalamic inputs is mechanistically unusual: Kawai, Lazar & Metherate (2007) showed it operates largely by raising the excitability of the myelinated thalamocortical axon itself, lowering the threshold for action-potential propagation, rather than by a classical terminal-release mechanism [26]. Because LP and ATN axons reach V2M through deep white matter and ascend to L1, basal-forebrain nicotinic tone (§3.3) is predicted to gate the *reliability* of these tuft-targeting inputs near threshold — a state-dependent switch on the thalamic feedback stream.

A complementary postsynaptic mechanism is pathway-selective in the same direction. Higher-order thalamic feedback to L2/3 apical tufts uniquely engages postsynaptic Group I mGluR (Grm1) to close two-pore K2P leak channels (Kcnk2 and relatives), boosting excitability and promoting NMDA spikes — a mechanism not invoked by other long-range inputs [29]. Since LP and ATN both deliver ~75% of their input to the apical tuft of V2M ttL5 neurons [24], this Grm1→Kcnk route is a strong candidate for local amplification of thalamic feedback, working alongside the presynaptic nicotinic gating of the same pathway. Both mechanisms predict that the thalamic tuft stream is selectively boosted under high cholinergic/high-glutamate (aroused, attentive) conditions.

**VISp (L5; feedforward; tuft-biased).** The V2M-projecting VISp population is L5 corticocortical (IT/PT) pyramidal neurons; their terminals carry the IC-type gate — GABA-B (Gabbr1/2), muscarinic M2/M4 (Chrm2/4), Group II mGluR autoreceptor (Grm2), and retrograde CB1 (Cnr1) all predicted to suppress release, with no nicotinic facilitation. Functionally VISp input is modest (0.93 pC) and tuft-biased on V2M ttL5 cells, so these gates act mainly on the distal apical compartment alongside the thalamic inputs.

**Local V2M (L5; recurrent; oblique-biased).** Recurrent input is the single largest source of charge onto ttL5 neurons (11.2 pC, 62% on oblique dendrites). It is IC-type and therefore the principal substrate for GABA-B- and muscarinic-mediated state-dependent gain control of the V2M column itself: muscarinic suppression of recurrent excitation (M1/M2 acting on Kcnq/SK postsynaptically and M2 presynaptically) is exactly the "afferent-dominant" mode of Gil et al. and Hasselmo, in which high ACh suppresses internal/associational drive while sparing (and, for the thalamic stream, enhancing) feedforward input.

**RSPg (L5/infragranular; feedback; distributed).** Granular retrosplenial (RSPd+RSPv) — distinct from the RSPagl that forms part of the V2M *target* (see §3.4). Feedback-type cortical input distributed across tuft, oblique, and basal compartments. Group II mGluR (Grm2) suppression is predicted to be frequency-selective, muting only high-frequency feedback bursts, by analogy with the mGluR2-mediated suppression of high-frequency corticothalamic/corticogeniculate feedback [27]. As a source area, RSg is the most densely noradrenergically innervated region of the entire neocortex (§3.3), so the RSPg projection neurons fire within an exceptionally NE-rich environment — a somatodendritic layer of control on this input that is unusual among the seven sources.

**ACA (L5/infragranular; feedback; oblique-biased).** Anterior cingulate provides large functional input (9.5 pC) concentrated on oblique dendrites, where it can gate FF→tuft information flow. IC-type presynaptic gating (GABA-B, M2, mGluR2, CB1) as for RSPg. In contrast to RSg, ACA receives the *lowest* noradrenergic innervation in neocortex, with dopaminergic fibres biased to its superficial layers [30] — so the ACA projection population sits in a low-NE, relatively DA-influenced environment at source.

**ORB (L2/3; FF-like axons; perisomatic/oblique-biased).** The distinguishing case. ORB→V2M neurons originate almost exclusively in L2/3 [24], and L2/3 cortical pyramidal axons are CB1-rich; the ORB terminal field in V2M is therefore predicted to be the most strongly endocannabinoid (CB1, Cnr1)-gated of the cortical inputs, on top of the shared GABA-B/M2/mGluR2 gates. ORB input is large (7.2 pC) and strongly perisomatic/oblique (57% oblique, 9% tuft), so presynaptic suppression here exerts leverage close to the ttL5 output, not just on distal integration. The L2/3 origin also predicts a different auxiliary-receptor complement from the L5-origin cortical inputs, a concrete contrast to test in the source-area expression data.

**LP and ATN (thalamic feedback; tuft-targeting).** Both are TC-type and overwhelmingly target the apical tuft (~75%). The thalamic relay transcriptome is α4β2-nicotinic-rich, consistent with nicotinic facilitation of these inputs via axonal/terminal mechanisms [26]; they are predicted GABA-B-insensitive [25]. Thalamic terminals are additionally subject to suppression by presynaptic A1 adenosine and 5-HT1/monoamine receptors — established for sensory-thalamic and retinothalamic terminals and synergistic between A1 and 5-HT1 — providing a sleep/arousal-graded brake on the thalamic stream. Postsynaptically the same tuft inputs are amplified through Grm1→K2P (§3.1). LP carries higher-order visual/contextual signals and ATN carries head-direction/vestibular signals (ATN being a node of the head-direction system with RSP), so the cholinergic-up/adenosine-down arousal switch on this tuft stream gates precisely the spatial-contextual feedback that the Rancz group has argued is central to V2M function.

### 3.3 Neuromodulatory source nuclei (the modulators themselves)

The seven Galloni inputs are glutamatergic. The neuromodulators that operate the gates above are released in V2M by separate subcortical afferents, which may be under-represented in rabies maps (volume transmission, weak monosynaptic uptake) but are established anatomically. Each modulatory terminal carries its own presynaptic **autoreceptor** controlling release — the first gate in the chain.

- **Basal forebrain cholinergic (→ ACh).** Medial septum/diagonal band: the VDB projects to cingulate and retrosplenial cortex; the HDB projects to cingulate, retrosplenial and visual cortex — so V2M and its medial cortical neighbours are well innervated. Cortical cholinergic fibres show birthdate-dependent inside-out lamination (early-born → deep layers, late-born → superficial) [32], predicting layer-biased ACh delivery across the ttL5 dendritic axis. ACh release is gated by presynaptic M2 (Chrm2) autoreceptors and H3 (Hrh3) heteroreceptors on cholinergic terminals. This is the source for the nicotinic facilitation of LP/ATN inputs (§3.1) and the muscarinic suppression of cortical inputs (§3.2).
- **Locus coeruleus noradrenergic (→ NE).** A frontal-to-occipital density gradient leaves visual cortex relatively NE-sparse overall, but with a striking dissociation among the V2M source areas: **RSg is the most densely NE-innervated neocortical region, with fibres throughout all layers, whereas ACA is the least NE-innervated, with DA biased to its superficial layers** [30]. NE release is gated by α2A (Adra2a) autoreceptors. This means the two cortical feedback sources to V2M (RSPg and ACA) are themselves under opposite noradrenergic control at their somata — a source-side asymmetry directly relevant to the project's NE focus.
- **Raphe serotonergic (→ 5-HT).** Cortical 5-HT innervation is laminarly organised and, in primary visual cortex, complementary to NE (dense where NE is sparse). 5-HT release is gated by 5-HT1B (Htr1b) autoreceptors. 5-HT1/monoamine receptors on thalamic terminals contribute the monoaminergic brake on LP/ATN inputs (§3.2).
- **VTA/midbrain dopaminergic (→ DA).** Sparse in visual cortex; among V2M sources, DA fibres are most evident in superficial ACA [30]. DA release is gated by D2 (Drd2) autoreceptors. Postsynaptic D1 (Drd1) on ttL5 dendrites would feed the cAMP-HCN axis of §1.3–1.4.

### 3.4 Section-specific caveats

The TC/IC dissociation [25] and the thalamic-terminal monoamine/adenosine brakes were established largely in somatosensory thalamocortical slices and sensory/retinothalamic terminals; applying them to V2M's LP/ATN-versus-cortical inputs is a cross-system inference ([E] → [I]). Layer of origin sets the projection neuron's transcriptome, but terminal receptor protein density can diverge from somatic mRNA — source-area MERFISH predicts the candidate, axonal protein in V2M is the ground truth. The seven pathways are not exhaustive: rabies also labelled lateral subcortical targets and additional thalamic/hypothalamic nodes (striatum, SC, PRT, LD, LGv, HY in the thesis dataset) at lower density, omitted here for focus. Finally, a naming hazard worth stating explicitly: the **RSPg** input (granular retrosplenial, RSPd+RSPv) is a *source* area and is distinct from **RSPagl**, the agranular-lateral retrosplenial region that forms part of the V2M *target* — consistent with the position that RSPagl is functionally visual (a V2M component) rather than canonically retrosplenial.

## 5. Scope notes / decisions for downstream use

**Expanded scope vs original handover.** The original handover brief designated ionotropic glutamate (Grin/Gria/Grik) and GABA-A (Gabra/b/g/d…) as out of scope. Per updated instruction, all commented-out config entries are now covered. Italicised rows in the table mark these entries to preserve visibility of which genes are currently inactive in the analysis pipeline.

**Evidence-level caveats for GPCR entries.** The [E:mPFC] or [E:PFC] tag applied to most DEEP-system rows reflects that quantitative pyramidal cell recordings for ACh/NE/5-HT modulation were performed predominantly in rodent PFC/prelimbic/infralimbic slices. The degree to which these mechanisms operate in mouse VISp/VISpm/VISam/RSPagl L2/3 and L5 requires confirmation; Allen scRNA-seq and MERFISH expression data are the necessary filter.

**Evidence for ionotropic glutamate and GABA-A entries.** NMDA and AMPA receptor physiology is so extensively documented in cortical pyramidal neurons that [P] tags apply universally but without single anchoring references — the field is covered by thousands of studies. Key functional principles (Q/R editing, Grin2A/2B switch, α5 tonic inhibition, Gabrg2 clustering) are textbook-level [P] findings.

**Muscle-type nicotinic subunits** (Chrna1, Chrnb1, Chrng, Chrnd, Chrne) have negligible CNS neuronal expression in mature animals; listed as a single row for config completeness.

**P2ry12** is a classical microglial marker; no pyramidal cell effect should be expected.

**MOR (Oprm1) in neocortex** is interneuron-dominant; the primary circuit effect of μ-agonists on pyramidal output is disinhibitory via interneuron suppression, not direct postsynaptic modulation.

**CB1 (Cnr1) expression caveat.** Allen and scRNA-seq data will show CB1 transcripts in pyramidal cells; these likely reflect axon terminal expression on pyramidal output fibres. The dominant functional location is presynaptic.

**RAMP1–3** are single-transmembrane chaperones, not GPCRs; they define the pharmacological identity of the CALCRL complex and are included because they appear in the config.

**5-HT2A in visual vs PFC cortex.** The net postsynaptic effect of 5-HT2A in PFC L5 (Gq → PKC → Nav inhibition + dendritic excitability suppression) may differ from RSPagl or VISp pyramidal neurons where receptor densities and co-expressed Nav/KCNQ stoichiometries differ. This cannot be extrapolated without direct recordings.

**BRIEF neuropeptide long tail.** All Tacr, Galr, Ntsr, Oxtr, Avpr, Mc, Cckar/Cckbr, Calcrl, Nmbr/Grpr, Npsr1, Npffr, Npbwr, Nmur, Ghsr, and Mtnr entries carry [I] tags. G-protein coupling assignments are established from expression systems; claims about M-current or SK suppression, GIRK activation, or HCN modulation are mechanistic inferences from coupling class, not measurements in pyramidal cells.

**Joint expression analysis.** The downstream analytical use of this companion is to ask: given that receptor X is expressed at level Y in pyramidal supertype Z (from Allen/Zhuang/Vizgen data), does the expression of its target conductances (Kcnq2/3/5, Kcnn2, Hcn1/2, etc. from excitability_genes.yaml) covary in a way that supports the predicted effector chain? That joint expression analysis is the primary quantitative deliverable this document is designed to support.
